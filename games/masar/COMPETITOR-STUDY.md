# MASAR Competitor Study

Snapshot date: 2026-07-21

## Legal and product boundary

MASAR may use the common tap-away mechanic and familiar puzzle conventions, but it must not reproduce a competitor's artwork, icon, exact screen composition, copy, sounds, level layouts, or branded visual identity. The product direction below is a synthesis of repeated market patterns plus original MASAR decisions.

## Market summary

The category has proven demand. Easybrain's 2D Arrow Puzzle reports 10M+ Google Play downloads and 891K reviews, while Popcore's broader 3D Tap Away reports 50M+ downloads and about 1.1M reviews. The strongest products keep the board dominant, make the rule understandable without text, add daily content, and sell optional help or cosmetics. The most repeated negative reviews concern disruptive ads, small targets, ambiguous paid ad removal, weak contrast, repeated boards, and rewards that do not feel useful.

## 1. Arrow Puzzle — Easybrain (2D)

Stores: [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.easybrain.arrow.puzzle.game), [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/arrow-puzzle-tap-away-game/id6755482314)

- Core loop: tap an arrow only when its forward path is free; clear the whole board.
- Presentation: the board occupies most of the screen. Level, remaining pieces, difficulty, and limited lives sit above it; hints sit below it.
- Progression: hundreds of handcrafted puzzles, rising difficulty, daily challenges, monthly trophies, and tournament-style competition.
- Monetization: interstitial advertising, rewarded help/lives, coin packs, bundles, and a no-ads purchase.
- Strengths: immediate comprehension, polished motion, strong store conversion, and a proven daily habit loop.
- Weaknesses found in store reviews: intrusive ads, small arrows and swipe/tap confusion, repeated puzzles, unclear tournament value, and a relatively expensive no-ads offer.
- MASAR decision: copy neither its artwork nor exact layout. Adopt direct-to-board entry, strong status hierarchy, daily play, and smooth exits. Improve touch size, avoid punitive life loss, and make competition rewards useful.

## 2. Arrow Puzzle 3D — Easybrain

Store: [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/arrow-puzzle-3d-arrows-game/id6777942798)

- Core loop: the same directional release rule on rotatable 3D shapes.
- Presentation: depth and rotation create spectacle while the board remains the focus.
- Retention: changing shapes and obstacles provide visual novelty beyond simple difficulty increases.
- Monetization: ads, optional rewarded hints/lives, and no-ads purchasing.
- Strengths: every level can look materially different; satisfying decomposition is easy to advertise.
- Weaknesses: higher production cost, larger app size, more camera friction, and complaints that paid ad removal does not remove every ad type.
- MASAR decision: remain 2D for clarity and production speed, but borrow the idea of visible level-to-level transformation through colors, patterns, and board silhouettes.

## 3. Tap Away — Popcore

Stores: [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.jctswbglsm.SwipeAway), [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tap-away-3d/id1568058543)

- Core loop: tap one-direction blocks to remove them from a 3D object.
- Presentation: large tactile blocks, dramatic exits, rotation, unlockable skins, and changing shapes.
- Retention: skins, themes, challenges, golden blocks, and long-term level progression.
- Monetization: frequent interstitials, rewarded bonuses, currencies, and cosmetics.
- Strengths: 50M+ downloads demonstrate the appeal of satisfying removal plus constant visual novelty.
- Weaknesses found in reviews: ads interrupt levels, poor arrow/block contrast in some skins, and reward moments that trigger more interruptions than delight.
- MASAR decision: use tactile exits, stage color changes, and cosmetic progression later. Do not interrupt an active board with advertising and never sacrifice arrow contrast for a skin.

## 4. Shift Out — Appindia

Store: [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.ai.arrows.puzzle.game)

- Core loop: a close 2D match to MASAR with open-path tapping and hints.
- Presentation: minimal board-first interface and persistent optional guide lines.
- Retention: 1,000+ levels, offline play, progressive difficulty, and regular level additions.
- Monetization: ads, rewarded actions, and a remove-ads purchase.
- Strengths: simple onboarding and helpful guide overlays reduce early confusion.
- Weaknesses found in reviews: distrust around what remove-ads covers and complaints about excessive advertising.
- MASAR decision: add a first-session visual tutorial and optional path guidance, but state purchases precisely and make paid ad removal remove every forced ad.

## 5. Arrow Puzzle — Omox

Store: [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.omox.arrowescape)

- Core loop: 1,000+ arrow escape puzzles.
- Meta systems: daily and weekly challenges, leaderboard, wheel, daily cards, lives, shields, tools, themes, and a planned battle arena.
- Strengths: the widest competitive and retention layer among the smaller direct competitors.
- Weaknesses found in reviews: cluttered boards, precision problems, overuse of short pieces, forced ads, and too many meta systems competing with the puzzle.
- MASAR decision: keep the daily leaderboard and useful rewards, reject the wheel/casino layer, and protect minimum target size on every generated board.

## 6. Arrow Puzzle: Tap Away — TactPeak

Store: [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.tactpeak.arrowpuzzle)

- Core loop: direct open-path arrow clearing.
- Progression: Easy, Normal, Hard, Expert, and Master tiers.
- Retention: daily challenges, golden stars, monthly trophies, and offline levels.
- Positioning: no timer and no countdown pressure.
- Strengths: difficulty language is immediately understandable and monthly goals give daily play a purpose.
- Risk: the proposition is highly generic without a distinct visual or competitive identity.
- MASAR decision: show difficulty clearly, keep Journey and Daily modes, and use the Clean Run score plus changing stage worlds as differentiation.

## 7. Arrow Escape: Maze Puzzle Out

Store: [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.arrow.puzzle)

- Core loop: direction-based release, collisions, hints, and increasingly complex boards.
- Market signal: 1M+ downloads but a materially weaker rating than the category leaders.
- Strengths: large acquisition reach and recognizable keyword targeting.
- Weaknesses: lower rating signals that acquisition alone does not protect retention or trust.
- MASAR decision: treat rating and session quality as primary product metrics, not just install volume.

## Shared winning pattern

1. Open directly into a playable board.
2. Give the board roughly 70–80% of useful screen space.
3. Keep level, difficulty, remaining pieces, and run quality visible above the board.
4. Put only one or two large tools below the board.
5. Make exits fast, tactile, and visually rewarding.
6. Increase difficulty gradually and introduce visual novelty separately from complexity.
7. Use a shared daily board and meaningful leaderboard rewards.
8. Keep play possible offline and make every touch target forgiving.

## MASAR's original synthesis

- One accent color per level, with the full atmosphere changing on progression.
- A Clean Run rating instead of lives: mistakes lower mastery but never stop play.
- Direct-to-board launch; the Journey portal remains available as progression context.
- Large square arrow targets with high-contrast borders and forgiving hit areas.
- Restart and Hint are the only launch tools; additional boosters require evidence from playtests.
- Daily score combines time, mistakes, and hints, so speed alone cannot dominate.
- No forced ad during a board. Future interstitials may appear only at natural breaks and at a controlled frequency.
- Rewarded ads are optional and exchange for a clear benefit. A no-ads purchase must remove all forced ads.

## Store presentation direction

The first two screenshots carry most of the conversion work. MASAR should lead with gameplay rather than its menu:

1. First screenshot: a nearly cleared high-contrast board with the promise `FIND THE OPEN PATH`.
2. Second screenshot: two consecutive stage colors with `A NEW WORLD EVERY LEVEL`.
3. Third screenshot: the daily score and ranking concept with `ONE PUZZLE. EVERY PLAYER.`
4. Icon: one bold arrow escaping a compact frame; no text and no competitor-like artwork.

Every screenshot and caption should be localized, with Arabic creative written for Arabic search behavior rather than translated word-for-word.
